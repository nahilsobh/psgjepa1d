\begin{abstract}
We iterate a JEPA-style world model on a 1-D longitudinal car whose exact
constant-jerk dynamics and discrete-optimum controller are known analytically.
The analytical optimum stops \SI{13.1}{\mm} shy of the wall on a fixed
170-scenario grid; the model's free-run rollout is measured against
\texttt{W.exact\_step} on 5000 random scenarios. Rounds tried:
grounding-only baseline, direct linear-decoder reconstruction, per-channel
recon weighting, embedding-dimension sweep, longer training with $10\times$
data, coverage rebalance, MLP intrinsic decoder, and a two-decoder scheme
that forces the encoder to be simultaneously linear- and MLP-decodable to
$(g, v, a)$. The linear-recon arm reaches the tightest \emph{gap} error
(\SI{33}{\mm} $k{=}10$; \SIrange{7.5}{9}{\mm} $k{=}1$ in the operating
regime, under the analytical ruler) but breaks the acceleration channel
(\SI{275}{\mm/\s^2}) and destroys the closed-loop controller (\SI{3}{\m}
safety needed). The two-decoder arm is the deployment winner: it preserves
all three physical channels, halves the paired controller excess
(\SI{2.79}{} to \SI{1.22}{\m}), and drops the tightest safety threshold
$8.6\times$ (from \SI{3}{\m} to \SI{0.35}{\m}). Aggregate model accuracy
is constrained by encoder information capacity that redistributes across
channels; the two-decoder scheme forces a broader distribution across
$(g, v, a)$ rather than concentrated gap-only tightness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The world is a single car approaching a wall on a 1-D track. State is
$(x, v, a)$; control is jerk $j$; bounds are $|a| \le \SI{8}{m/s^2}$,
$|j| \le \SI{20}{m/s^3}$, $v \ge 0$. Discretisation is $\Delta t = \SI{0.1}{\s}$.

\emph{Exact dynamics.} \texttt{psgjepa1d/world.py:exact\_step} subdivides each
tick on events (acceleration hitting $\pm A_{\max}$, velocity hitting 0) and
integrates the constant-jerk closed form on each sub-interval; verified to
$7 \times 10^{-7}$ vs a fine RK integrator.

\emph{Analytical optimum.} \texttt{world.optimal\_stop} enumerates coast
lengths and terminal-brake plans that land $(v{=}0, a{=}0)$ exactly. On the
170-scenario grid (initial $v_0 \in \{3, 4, \ldots, 14\}$~m/s, wall
$\in \{6, 7, \ldots, 22\}$~m subject to feasibility), the mean stopping excess
is $\SI{13.13}{\mm}$. This is the \emph{ruler}.

\emph{Non-dimensional scales.} $T_0 = A_{\max}/J_{\max} = \SI{0.4}{\s}$,
$L_0 = A_{\max}^3/J_{\max}^2 = \SI{1.28}{\m}$,
$V_0 = A_{\max}^2/J_{\max} = \SI{3.2}{\m/\s}$. Physics normalisation
divides $(g, v, a)$ by $(L_0, V_0, A_{\max})$ and jerks by $J_{\max}$. The only
dimensionless parameter is $\Pi = V_{\max} J_{\max}/A_{\max}^2 = 4.375$.

\section{Data}

$N = 2\times 10^6$ windows of $T{=}7$ frames each are synthesised offline from a
7-stream stratified sampler (\texttt{data.py:gen\_windows}). Each window: sample
$(g_0, v_0, a_0)$ from the mixture; then apply a piecewise-constant jerk
sequence (initial jerk $\sim$U$(-20, 20)$; at each subsequent step, resample
with probability 0.35 else hold); roll forward with \texttt{exact\_step}. State
storage uses gap remaining ($g_0 - x$), not absolute position. Futures are
machine-zero vs the physics -- verified by \texttt{tests/test\_world.py}.

The default 7-stream mixture (fractions in parens): \emph{bulk} (32\%, uniform
box), \emph{fast} (10\%, $v \in [11,14]$), \emph{final} (8\%, small gap
low $v$), \emph{brake} (9\%, $a<-2$), \emph{accel} (6\%, $a>2$), \emph{bound}
(13\%, gap near analytical minimum brake distance), \emph{edge} (22\%, $v\approx14$,
gap $\approx 0.9$--$1.2\times$ brake distance).

\section{Model and training}

\texttt{JEPA1D} = encoder $\phi: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^D$
(MLP 3--512--512--$D$, GELU); residual action-conditioned predictor $\pi_\theta:
\mathbb{R}^{D+1} \to \mathbb{R}^D$ (MLP with $z_{t+1} = z_t + \Delta$);
optional linear intrinsic decoder $\psi: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^3$
(default) or MLP $D\to h\to 3$. Grounding heads (from PSG-JEPA): static
$H_s$ predicts $s_t$; transition $H_d$ predicts multi-horizon
$\Delta q_{t:t+k}$; optional velocity $H_v$ predicts $v_t$.

Loss:
$\mathcal{L} = \|z_{t+1} - \pi(z_t, u_t)\|^2 + \lambda_r R(\phi(S)) + \lambda_g
L_{\text{ground}} + \lambda_p \|\psi(\phi(S)) - S\|^2$
where $R$ is \texttt{sigreg} (sliced characteristic-function regulariser;
$\lambda_r = 0.09$), $\lambda_g = 0.1$ (paper value), and $\lambda_p$ (recon
weight) is 0 or 1.0. Optimiser: AdamW, $\text{lr} = 10^{-3}$
(the config value $3\times 10^{-3}$ diverges), weight decay $10^{-3}$, batch
4096, cosine schedule over 30 epochs. Predictor target is
\texttt{.detach()}-ed to avoid latent collapse.

\emph{Verify method.} 5000 random $(g_0, v_0, a_0, u_{0:9})$; encode $s_0$,
free-run the predictor 10 steps, decode each step, compare to
\texttt{exact\_step} in physical units. Decoded via an MLP post-hoc decoder
trained with 90\%/10\% split for 100 epochs on 2.4\,M pairs (channel-wise
val MSE reported).

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{full\_recon} halves the 10-step gap MAE (61 $\to$ 33 mm) but

\subsection{Diagnostics}

Three data-related hypotheses were tested on \texttt{full\_v2} and
\texttt{full\_recon} without retraining.

\emph{H1 (coverage).} \textbf{Confirmed.} The $\sim$20 mm aggregate is not a
uniform floor. Some regions are already at 8--14 mm; others are at 40--60 mm.
The heaviest contributor is $v_\text{lo} \times \text{gap}_\text{hi}$
(slow car far from wall), covered mainly by the \emph{bulk} stream (32\%) and
\emph{accel} (6\%).

\emph{H2 (jerk-sequence mismatch).} \textbf{Rejected.} Training uses 35\%
resample rate; verify uses independent per-step jerks. Comparing
\texttt{full\_v2} at $k{=}10$: independent jerks 61 mm, training-style jerks
63 mm. Essentially no effect. The predictor is one-step and does not depend
on jerk-history correlation.

\emph{H3 (quantity).} \textbf{Rejected at same-epoch budget.} 10$\times$ data
under-trained; $k{=}10$ gap MAE went 33 $\to$ 107 mm. A fair test would need
$\sim$100 epochs on the 20\,M cache ($\sim$8 h GPU).

\subsection{Controller closed-loop}
\label{sec:controller}

Beyond world-model accuracy, we evaluated closed-loop stopping on the
170-scenario grid. Controller mirrors \texttt{world.optimal\_stop} using the
model in place of the physics: roll a coast plan forward in the model, fan
trim jerks at each step, pick the plan with predicted excess closest to a
target excess (\texttt{= safety} by default; ``aim tight''). The tightest-safe
margin is the smallest safety at which crashes $\le 2$/170.

\emph{The a-channel matters a lot for control.} \texttt{full\_recon} has the
tightest \emph{gap} accuracy but the worst controller performance
(safety $\SI{3}{\m}$, $\SI{2.78}{\m}$ excess). Its broken $a$-channel
(\SI{275}{\mm/\s^2}) corrupts the brake-distance grid (indexed by $v, a$),
so plans are systematically mis-scored no matter what safety buffer we add.
A dedicated experiment with $\texttt{target\_excess} = \texttt{safety} + 1.5\sigma_g$
on \texttt{full\_recon} confirmed this: it did not reduce crashes at tight
margins.

\emph{The two-decoder arm resolves this.} \texttt{full\_recon\_two} is the
tightest-safe controller across every arm we trained: $\SI{0.35}{\m}$ safety,
$\SI{1.25}{\m}$ excess, 1 crash out of 170. Its acceleration channel (best
across all arms, $\SI{23}{\mm/\s^2}$) fixes the brake-grid mis-scoring.

\texttt{full\_recon} and \texttt{full\_recon\_two} succeed:

The controller-level moral: for a safety-margin-sensitive workload, \emph{all

tightest on gap alone is unusable if $a$ is broken, because the brake grid is
the controller's link between predicted state and physical stopping distance.

\section{Interpretation}

\subsection{Encoder capacity is the constraint}

Table \ref{tab:capacity} summarises the accuracy budget for the recon
variants. The encoder is 3--512--512--$D$ with $D \in \{64, 128, 192\}$.
Doubling $D$ from 64 to 128 delivered essentially no aggregate improvement on
$k{=}1$ gap (24 vs 19 mm, within seed variance). More striking, the linear
decoder's per-channel val MSE at $D{=}64$ redistributes across loss
configurations without shrinking in total:

Total budget is $\sim 10^{-3}$ across configs; the loss configuration steers
\emph{which channel} pays. Linear-decode-plus-recon forces gap and $v$ tight
and sacrifices $a$. MLP-decode-plus-recon preserves gap and $a$ and
sacrifices $v$. \textbf{Two decoders enforced together
(\texttt{full\_recon\_two})} give the lowest total budget of any arm
($6.4 \times 10^{-4}$) and the best acceleration channel by an order of
magnitude, at the cost of the $g$-only concentration seen in
\texttt{full\_recon}. This is the first arm that keeps all three channels
usable simultaneously.

\subsection{What we tried since; what remains}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two-decoder training. \emph{Done; works.}}
      \texttt{full\_recon\_two} preserves all three channels (best $a$ across
      all arms), gives the smallest post-hoc-decoder total-channel error, and
      $8.6\times$ tightens the closed-loop safety threshold ($\SI{3}{\m}
      \to \SI{0.35}{\m}$). Modest cost: $k{=}10$ gap MAE $33 \to 58$~mm.
\item \textbf{Physics-structured latent.} Reserve three latent dimensions
      explicitly for $(g, v, a)$ via an alignment loss; leave the rest as
      free features. Untried; may push the $k{=}10$ gap MAE back down while
      keeping the two-decoder channel breadth.
\item \textbf{Bigger encoder + longer training + more data, together.}
      $D{=}256$, 100+ epochs, 20\,M windows. Estimated 8--10 h GPU. Would
      test whether the current $\sim$30 mm gap floor is a modelling choice
      or a compute-budget choice.
\item \textbf{Uncertainty-aware controller.} Already tried on
      \texttt{full\_recon} where it did not help because $a$ was broken.
      Now that \texttt{full\_recon\_two} has a working $a$, a
      $\texttt{target\_excess} = \texttt{safety} + k\sigma$ policy could
      push its tightest-safe margin below \SI{0.35}{\m} and its excess below
      \SI{1.25}{\m}. Untried on the two-decoder arm.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item Verify samples $(g_0, v_0, a_0)$ uniformly over the box but only
      $u_{0:9}$ independently -- physically-implausible trajectories
      (car passing through the wall) are counted. The
      \emph{v\_hi}$\times$\emph{gap\_lo}$\times k{=}10$ cell in
      Table \ref{tab:regions} is largely such trajectories.
\item Closed-loop controller uses aim-tight scoring
      (\texttt{target\_excess = safety}). The uncertainty-buffered variant
      was only tested on \texttt{full\_recon} (where it did not help because
      the $a$-channel was broken). It has not been re-run on
      \texttt{full\_recon\_two}, which has a working $a$-channel and may
      benefit substantially.
\item Every arm uses the same 30-epoch cosine schedule at $\text{lr}{=}10^{-3}$
      except \texttt{full\_d192\_e60\_recon}. Hyperparameter sensitivity was
      not swept.
\item No baseline against an oracle model with $\psi = \text{identity}$ and
      the true predictor; that would isolate encoder loss from predictor loss.
\end{itemize}

\section{Bottom line}

Two winners for two different questions.

\textbf{For raw gap-prediction accuracy:} \texttt{full\_recon} (linear
recon), \SI{33}{\mm} $k{=}10$ gap MAE and \SIrange{7.5}{9}{\mm} $k{=}1$ in
the operating regime. It beats the \SI{13}{\mm} analytical ruler cell-by-cell
in mid/high-$v$ scenarios, but is unusable as a controller world-model
because its acceleration channel collapses (\SI{275}{\mm/\s^2}).

\textbf{For deployment as a controller world-model:}
\texttt{full\_recon\_two} (two decoders enforced simultaneously). It gives
up the concentrated $k{=}1$ gap peak (\SI{7.5}{\mm} $\to$ \SI{10}{\mm}
best-cell) in exchange for preserving all three channels. Closed-loop
consequences are dramatic: safety threshold drops $8.6\times$ (\SI{3}{\m}
$\to$ \SI{0.35}{\m}), paired excess halves (\SI{2.79}{\m} $\to$

The encoder-capacity story from the original report still holds: at fixed
$D{=}64$, precision redistributes across $(g, v, a)$ rather than growing.
What changed is that the two-decoder objective steers the encoder to a
broadly-distributed operating point rather than a gap-concentrated one -- and
for a controller that needs all three channels through the brake-distance
grid, broadly-distributed wins.
